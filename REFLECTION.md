They have to give only few minutes to write it down and check it even on the train or bus.

Q.3 In the loop, on the loop, out of the loop: where was your judgment actually needed?
---
When I gave AI Studio my prompt, the result came out almost exactly how I wanted. I still checked the details and the interface to make sure everything worked and made sense for students. For my product, I think the student should be in the loop when looking at the tasks have been done or result. The tool can help check the text and tasks, but the student should decide whether the tips or reminders are useful or correct.

I think some simple things can be on the loop or even out of the loop. For example, basic checking, displaying results, or recording completed tasks can happen automatically because they are simple and easy to correct if something goes wrong.

Overall, I don't think a human needs to check every single action. The AI can handle the simple and repetitive parts, while the student should still have the final judgment when it really matters.

Q.4 What did it build that you never sketched?
---
One thing AI Studio built that I didn't really sketch was the extra loading state and some small interface details. I was mainly thinking about the main screen and the text-checking function, so I didn't think much about what would happen while the result was loading. I noticed this during the build when I saw the app working. I think I could have caught it earlier if I had thought more about the different states of the interface, not just what the final screen should look like.

Q.5 Learning pointers for the organisational context
---
We should check what AI actually builds before deployment like someone should review the generated structure before an app goes live.
Set clear rules for data before prompting: Since my product involves students entering text, an organisation should decide what information employees are allowed to put into AI tools and make this clear before they start building.
Keep ownership and usage records: My experience showed me that it is possible to use a tool without fully thinking about everything happening behind the interface, so the organisation should record who built each app, what AI tools it uses, and who is responsible for it after the original builder leaves.
